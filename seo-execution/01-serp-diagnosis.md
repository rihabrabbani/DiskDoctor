# SERP diagnosis — `hard drive repair` / `data recovery` (13 Aug 2026)

## What we could measure from here

A logged-out US-IP incognito screenshot was not possible from this environment. The GSC signature is still decisive, and live HTML was fetched.

## Evidence

- `hard drive repair`: 50,234 impressions, **0 clicks**, avg position **1.08**, `search_type: web`, almost entirely **desktop**.
- `data recovery`: 9,928 impressions, **1 click**, avg position **1.29**.
- Desktop: 97.5% of impressions, 0.06% CTR, avg pos 5.66.
- Mobile: 2.4% of impressions, 3.44% CTR, avg pos 23 — a normal small local site.
- A genuine US #1 blue link for “hard drive repair” would produce thousands of clicks in 90 days, not zero.
- The `http://www.diskdoctorsamerica.com/` URL still holds 59,194 impressions at pos 1.25 while HTTPS holds 7,208 at pos 19.67. The 301 to HTTPS works today.

## Decision (do not chase the head terms)

This is **not** a title-tag problem worth an afternoon of “we’re #1 for hard drive repair.” Position 1 with 0.00% CTR is a SERP-module / AI Overview / legacy-HTTP attribution artifact, not a clickable listing we can monetise by rewriting the homepage H1.

**Strategy:** abandon national head terms (`hard drive repair`, `data recovery`) as traffic goals. Reinvest in:

1. Near-me and city-modified queries in MD / DC / NoVA (already 3–14% CTR from positions 13–18).
2. On-site conversion of the ~26 organic visits we actually get.
3. GBP (client-gated) as the likely source of current leads.
4. Problem-symptom long-tail for AEO (`hard drive clicking`, RAID 5 two-drive failure).

If a later US incognito session shows a real blue link with a bad snippet, revisit titles — they are being rewritten anyway in Phase 2. Do not build content volume against the 50k-impression mirage.
